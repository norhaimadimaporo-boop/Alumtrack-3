CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

Alumni are considered a vital asset for every university because they reflect the institution's achievements and serve as partners in the ongoing growth of their Alma mater. These individuals are crucial to educational institutions, as they are often tracked through Alumni Tracer Systems, which are designed to track graduates’ career paths and determine if their employment corresponds with their undergraduate/graduate degrees. Effective alumni tracking enables institutions to refine curricula, strengthen industry partnerships, and provide better career support for future students. The implementation of systems like AlumTrack holds societal significance by supporting the Sustainable Development Goal 8: Decent Work and Economic Growth. This goal is achieved by highlighting the career paths and employment status of graduates, thereby creating opportunities for professional networking and inspiring current students to pursue meaningful careers. Furthermore, this process adds value by improving efficiency in alumni tracking and fostering collaboration, ultimately turning the alumni network into a resource for both individual growth and university progress.

The need for this research stems from practical issues related to traditional data management at Mindanao State University, Main Campus. Although the office of alumni relations and career center (OARCC)  maintains databases for the hundreds of graduates produced annually, these traditional and manual processes often limit the data and make it difficult to track information and reach out to alumni effectively. A key practical issue is that the gathered data is not always accessible to students who have not yet graduated. To address these challenges, the project, “AlumTrack: Alumni Tracing System and Online Platform for Mindanao State University-Main Campus” is proposed as a solution. This web-based system is designed to function as an online platform where alumni can register, create, and update their profiles. This solution will enable administrators to efficiently manage alumni data, verify information, and monitor engagement without relying on manual record-keeping. Additionally, the system will use the collected alumni information to visually display the location of the companies or places where they currently work.

The research gap identified in the existing system is the reliance on manual processes, which results in inefficient data management and limited engagement. While institutions with established tracking systems benefit from improved communication and the ability to assess career alignment, MSU-Main Campus faces the ongoing challenge of keeping track of its graduates and maintaining strong, lifelong relationships with them once they leave the university due to these outdated methods. The project "AlumTrack" directly addresses this gap by offering a centralized, web-based platform. Implementing AlumTrack will enhance the alumni association's communication, organization, and services. It provides alumni with an accessible and reliable way of staying connected with the university, thereby strengthening the university-alumni bond through technology and fostering active participation. This system aims to support the university administration by providing updated data necessary for accreditation, program evaluation, and institutional development.

1.1 Project Context

The capstone project, “AlumTrack: Alumni Tracing System and Online Platform for Mindanao State University - Main Campus,” is a software development initiative focused on designing and developing an online, web-based tracing system. This platform aims to collect and manage data from alumni to trace their current statuses after graduation and to visually display the location of their current employment on a map. 

The system will specifically be conducted and implemented at Mindanao State University- Main Campus (MSU-Main Campus). The core concern of the project is to address the practical issues related to traditional and manual data management currently employed by the office of alumni relations and career center (OARCC). The reliance on these manual processes is identified as a research gap that results:Outdated or incomplete information. Difficulty tracking alumni career paths. Inefficient communication between the university and graduates. Limited access for current students who need alumni insights.

Therefore, to address these issues, the project “AlumTrack: Alumni Tracing System and Online Platform for MSU-Main Campus” is introduced. It is a web-based system designed to: Allow alumni to register, create, and update their profiles online. Help administrators manage data more efficiently. Display alumni employment locations using Geographic Information System (GIS) mapping. Strengthen MSU’s connection with its graduates through an accessible and reliable online portal.

The system provides updated data necessary for program evaluation, accreditation, and institutional development. Furthermore, AlumTrack contributes to the Sustainable Development Goal 8: Decent Work and Economic Growth by highlighting career paths and fostering professional networking opportunities. The key stakeholders involved and benefited by this project include; Alumni relations office and career center (OARCC), University Administration, who gain organizational and monitoring support, and the Alumni (graduates of MSU-Main Campus).




	Regarding its development and timeline, the system development will be conducted as a web system utilizing technologies such as Visual Studio code, XAMPP or MySQL, Figma, JavaScript, HTML, and CSS, which is designed to resolve the inherent problems of the manual system. Although the project is currently yet to be proposed, it is expected to be finished by the end of the school year 2025-2026.

2.1.1 Alumni Commitment and Loyalty

According to pedro et .al (2020), Alumni commitment is defined as the emotional attachment and behavioral loyalty of the alumni held toward their alma mater. For every university worldwide, maintaining their connections to their graduates/alumni is considered a strategic approach, as these connections can sometimes offer multiple opportunities and benefits, including a sense of belonging and connections among the alumni community. The key drivers of this loyalty include the perceived qualities of education, positive student experiences, and strong faculty relationships (Snijders et al., 2019). And furthermore, Cownie and Gallo (2020) emphasized the role of fostering a sense of gratitude among alumni, highlighting its connection to increased engagement.

As technology has emerged as a powerful tool for fostering this commitment by facilitating communication, engagement, and a sense of community (Sutrave et al., 2020). For platforms like the Alumni Tracer and Engagement Hub, providing a system for interaction, networking, and shared experiences has the potential to nurture this sense of community and strengthen the alumni-alma mater bond (Rubejes-Silva, 2024).

2.1.2 Graduate Tracer Studies(GTS)

According to Cuandra et. al. (2019), A tracer study is recognized as a very powerful tool that academic institutions use to provide valuable information for evaluating the outcomes of education and training. Graduate tracer studies (GTS) are a highly effective and critical tool for Education institutions to measure the success and relevance of their academic programs by evaluating and looking directly at the career paths and performance of their graduates. 

Cuandra et. al emphasize that tracing graduates and collecting their data through GTS is one of the documentary requirements of higher education accrediting bodies, such as AACCUP (Accrediting Agency of Chartered Colleges and Universities in the Philippines), etc. or for accreditation of the colleges. As they use the graduates or Alumni's data, it provides quantitative and qualitative evidence of an institution's success and effectiveness of its curriculum. This data is critical for accreditation because it allows external evaluators (like AACCUP) to assess outcomes rather than just inputs.

2.1.3 Geographical Information System (GIS)

According to Rusko et. al.(2010), the Geographical Information System (GIS) is defined as the world’s most advanced digital brain for managing location-based information. It is fundamentally a computer-based tool and an analysis tool that goes far beyond simply making maps. Its core purpose is to take the complex, real world and organize it, acting as a crucial facility for collecting, storing, transforming, retrieving, and displaying spatial data. Think of GIS as a digital system that answers the core questions of geography: It tracks the exact position of everything using coordinates, what those things are (their attributes, such as soil moisture, type of vegetation, or ownership of land), and how they are connected (their spatial interrelations, also known as topology). This organizational power means GIS is constantly performing data input, storage, manipulation, and detailed reporting to form its database, which can be structured using formats like the grid-based raster or the coordinate point vector.

Rusko et. al.(2010) emphasizes that the real power of GIS lies in its role as a crucial integrator. The system integrates all kinds of information and applications with a geographic component into one manageable system. This integrated analysis is vital because it allows the GIS to support a decision-making process. Whether designing maps for water, wastewater, and storm water systems or applying Digital Elevation Models (DEM) to accurately represent terrain for hydrologic models, GIS technology empowers organizations to make better and informed decisions based on all relevant factors, with the ultimate intention of improving everyday life. However, this sophisticated tool is not automatic: it functions only when the requisite expertise is available, the data is compiled, and the programs are properly modified to suit the application. Finally, we must treat the system's output as a guiding framework, acknowledging that the digital representation of geography is not equal to the geography itself, and always involves some degree of approximation.

2.1.4 Alumni Tracer and Engagement Hub

According to Rubejes-Silva (2024), the underlying theory for utilizing online platforms in alumni relations posits that a dedicated Technology-Driven Platform is the necessary strategic tool to effectively manage and sustain the connection between universities and their former students. This approach theorizes that persistent institutional challenges in fostering meaningful alumni connections can be resolved by deploying a specialized web-based platform, exemplified by the Alumni Tracer and Engagement Hub. The platform is fundamentally designed with a dual objective: first, to enable the "tracing" aspect by securely maintaining contact and data about alumni, and second, to drive Alumni Engagement through interaction and communication tools. The platform must function successfully as a bridge the gap mechanism, ensuring that the institution maintains access to its alumni while providing resources and fostering a connection that helps build a thriving alumni network.

For this online platform to realize its potential as an effective tracing and engagement mechanism, it must adhere to rigorous standards of quality, both internal and external. Rubejes-Silva (2024) emphasizes that the theoretical success of the platform is validated through a comprehensive evaluation framework that integrates technical soundness and user perception. The technical viability, assessed using McCall's Software Quality Model, established that the system possesses a strong technical foundation, achieving high marks for internal quality factors like security, code clarity, and user-friendliness. Crucially, the practical effectiveness of the platform and its ability to fulfill the tracing and connecting objectives is assessed through a user-centric lens, utilizing the ISO/IEC 25010 model. Positive user feedback on core functionalities like functional completeness, performance, and learnability confirms that the platform is technically capable of supporting its intended purpose of strengthening alumni connections.

However, the theory concludes that the long-term success of the platform for alumni tracing and engagement hinges on continually optimizing the user experience based on specific human feedback. While the system's technical foundation proved strong, the evaluation identified critical areas directly impacting the alumni's willingness to use and trust the tracing system. These included deficiencies in user interface aesthetics, the clarity of element purpose, and particularly user perceptions of data security. Therefore, according to the study,  the final theoretical imperative is that to truly become a valuable tool capable of ensuring long-term technical maintainability and connection strengthening, the platform must proactively implement recommendations for optimizing the user experience and bolstering security measures, thereby ensuring the alumni feel safe and comfortable using the platform for tracing and engagement purposes.

2.4 Technical Background

The operational environment at Mindanao State University - Main Campus’s Office of Alumni Relations currently handles a substantial alumni network spanning decades of graduates, requiring management of thousands of individual records while serving multiple stakeholder groups including university administration, current students, and the alumni association. This extensive scope necessitates efficient data management and communication systems to maintain meaningful connections across this diverse community.

The current operational procedures involve manual data handling through spreadsheet-based record keeping and paper form processing for new graduate information. Staff members conduct alumni tracking through basic email surveys and social media monitoring, while communication relies on broadcast-style announcements across generic platforms. The reporting process for accreditation requires manual data compilation from multiple disconnected sources, creating significant administrative overhead and potential for data inconsistency.

Several critical operational challenges persist under this framework. Data integrity issues emerge from reliance on static collection methods, resulting in outdated graduate information and incomplete employment tracking. Communication inefficiencies limit engagement quality, while reporting delays impact institutional assessment capabilities. The absence of centralized coordination mechanisms further complicates relationship management across the alumni community, affecting both short-term engagement and long-term institutional planning.

The AlumTrack system addresses these operational gaps through an integrated technical solution featuring self-service profile management, automated tracking capabilities, and targeted communication tools, This web-based platform will incorporate GIS mapping for visual alumni distribution analysis and centralized reporting functions to support accreditation requirements. By implementing this comprehensive system, the university aims to transform alumni relations into a dynamic, data driven function that enhances graduate connectivity and supports institutional development goals through reliable, real-time information access.

3.4 Testing

3.4.1 respondents 

The respondents for the AlumTrack system's evaluation are selected based on the main end-users of the system: the MSU Alumni Association/University Administration staff and the MSU Alumni themselves. These participants are chosen because they will use the system to manage, monitor, and update records or to access online platforms for registration and profile updates. The selection of a representative sample ensures the collected data accurately reflects the user experience of the target population. The researchers may also include current students, as they are part of the users who will benefit from the data to identify professional opportunities.

3.4.2 procedure

The testing procedure is systematically divided into two parts: Alpha Testing and Beta Testing. 




Alpha Testing

During the Alpha Testing, the researchers simulate various usage scenarios, such as registering an alumnus or tracing a status to identify any functionality issues, system bugs, or usability concerns. Any problems found are addressed and fixed before the external testing phase. 




Beta Testing 

The Beta Testing phase involves visiting the end-users (the alumni and admin) and presenting the finalized web application for them to use in a real-world setting. This allows the researchers to gather valuable, on-hand feedback regarding the application's usability, overall performance, user experience, and any suggested improvements.

3.4. 3 tools ( questionnaire)

Software Usability Scale (SUS)

The primary tool for measuring the perceived usability of the AlumTrack system is the System Usability Scale (SUS). This is a widely accepted, 10-item questionnaire that provides a quantifiable measure of user experience. Users rate their agreement with statements using a 5-point Likert scale. The results from testing key tasks, such as registration and profile updating, are computed into a single usability score to confirm the system meets its objective of being user-friendly.
